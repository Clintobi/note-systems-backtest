    How a note works

    • You lend $1,000 against one stock for up to 8 weeks. Every Friday the note checks the official closing price. If the stock hasn't fallen more than 35%, you're paid $3.75 (at the reference rate; the real rate is set by demand).
    • If the stock is back at or above where it started, the note ends early and your $1,000 comes back.
    • If it's down more than 35% at the very last check, you're paid in the stock instead of cash. That's the risk the payments are for.
    • The other side is SHIELD: someone who owns the stock. They fund those payments, and if the crash happens they hand over their shares and get $1,000 in cash. Switch sides at the top to see it from their seat.

    All notes on this stock

    Uses the standing template in the Note Systems docs: 8 weekly checks at the official Friday close, a 65% barrier, and the reference coupon of 0.50% a week. The real coupon floats between 0.25% and 1.25% a week depending on how many people want each side. The protocol keeps 25% of each coupon, so the COUPON side receives $3.75 a week on $1,000. The SHIELD side pays the full $5 on each paid check plus 0.75% ($7.50) when the note starts. Series created under the earlier fee schedule use 15% and 0.25%. A check where the stock is more than 35% down pays nothing, and there is no catch-up later. Prices are real daily closes adjusted for splits and dividends. One note was started every Friday from 2015 to 2025, 3,683 in total. This is a backtest, not a forecast, and not financial advice. The notes are still on Note Systems testnet.
